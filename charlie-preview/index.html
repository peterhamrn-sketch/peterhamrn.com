Little Mini: Who Watches the Watchdog?

Introducing Charlie, Chief of Security

Every important operation needs someone in charge of security. The White House has the Secret Service. Banks have surveillance cameras. Airports have entire departments dedicated to keeping an eye on things.

Our house has Charlie.

Charlie is a dachshund. A fifteen-year-old long-haired dachshund, to be precise, although I suspect he'd prefer that we not discuss his age in public. He is also the self-appointed Chief of Security, Director of Household Activities, and Senior Vice President of Treat Distribution.

His qualifications are extensive. He knows precisely when someone opens the refrigerator, has an uncanny ability to determine whether a person sitting down might have a lap available, and takes his responsibility for supervising household activities very seriously. His hearing may not be what it once was, but that hasn't diminished his enthusiasm for keeping an eye on everyone.

But Charlie wasn't always the confident little fellow who runs our household. In fact, his story began in circumstances that were anything but happy.

Before Charlie Came Home

Charlie was born on March 7, 2011. At some point in his early life, things went terribly wrong. He was abandoned in a foreclosed home.

Fortunately, the people at Paw-Affection Dachshund Rescue stepped in. Charlie received the medical care he needed, including surgery, and spent time in foster care while he recovered.

He was safe. But being safe and feeling safe aren't necessarily the same thing. I don't know everything that happened to him before he was rescued, and Charlie isn't talking. But whatever he experienced left its mark.

Charlie was frightened, defensive, and not particularly interested in trusting people. He had learned that the world could be a dangerous place, and he wasn't about to forget it just because somebody offered him a treat.

Homecoming?

We adopted Charlie in December 2019. He didn't arrive as one of those instantly affectionate dogs who leaps into your arms and showers you with kisses. Charlie had opinions. Strong ones. And some of those opinions involved keeping people at a safe distance.

He was fear-aggressive, particularly when he felt threatened or uncertain. We knew he needed patience, consistency, and time.

For the rest of that first day, we stayed in the kitchen, and Charlie stayed in the living room. Whenever someone walked past the living room, Charlie barked. Sometimes he growled, too. We spent that first night with a baby gate across the hallway, mostly because we were concerned Charlie might decide to camp out in front of the only bathroom and bite anyone who tried to use it.

Not exactly the warm, fuzzy homecoming you see in adoption commercials.

Apparently, everyone managed to sleep through the night without incident. I was the first one up the next morning, so I went to check on Charlie. He was still in the living room, right where we'd left him.

I asked if he needed to go outside, and to my surprise, he promptly trotted to the back door. I managed to pick up his leash, which he was still wearing because nobody had been able to get close enough to remove it, and took him outside to take care of his business. When we came back inside, I asked if he was hungry. Charlie went straight to his food bowl, apparently understanding the question perfectly.

And just like that, my career as Charlie's Chief Chef had begun.

Later that day, about twenty-four hours after Charlie had come home, I was sitting down watching football. And there was Charlie, sound asleep on my lap. The little dog who had every reason to distrust the world had decided that one human could be trusted. I don't know whether Charlie understood what a permanent home meant. But I like to think that somewhere between breakfast and the football game, he began to figure it out.

The Shadow

That was nearly seven years ago. Charlie has since developed a rather firm policy concerning my whereabouts. Wherever I go, Charlie believes he should go too. If I move to another room, he follows. If I sit down, he investigates the available seating arrangements. If I get up, he wants to know why. Privacy, in Charlie's opinion, is a highly overrated concept.

The Important Things in Life

Charlie has a few priorities. Treats rank very high. Sunny places to sleep are also important. And despite his age, he occasionally experiences a sudden burst of enthusiasm that sends him racing around the house.

Zoomies.

There is something particularly entertaining about watching a senior dachshund suddenly remember that he has places to be. One moment he's a distinguished elderly gentleman enjoying a peaceful afternoon. The next he's a low-flying missile with very short legs.

And then, just as suddenly, the excitement is over. Time for a nap, preferably somewhere close to me.

Chief of Security

As the Little Mini project grew, our collection of monitoring systems became increasingly elaborate. Computers were watching other computers. Websites were checking whether other websites were working. Alerts were being sent when something went wrong.

And eventually we reached the inevitable question:

Who watches the watchdog?

The answer, of course, had been living in our house all along.

Charlie.

He had been supervising the household for years without requiring a software update, an internet connection, or a monthly subscription.

Admittedly, his monitoring system has a few limitations. It occasionally goes offline for extended naps, and its operating costs include a substantial number of treats.

But it has one advantage over every computer I've ever owned. Charlie doesn't care whether the Wi-Fi is working. He just wants to know where I am.

The Best Part of the Story

It's easy to look at Charlie today and see a funny little dachshund who likes treats, sunshine, football naps, and keeping track of his people. But I sometimes think about the frightened dog who was found abandoned in that foreclosed house.

The one who needed surgery. The one who had to learn that people could be trusted again. And I think about the people at Paw-Affection Dachshund Rescue who gave him that chance.

Without them, there wouldn't have been that first football game with Charlie asleep on my lap. There wouldn't have been nearly seven years of having a little shadow follow me from room to room. There wouldn't have been all the zoomies, the sunny afternoon naps, or the countless moments when a small dog somehow managed to occupy an enormous part of our lives.

Charlie may be our Chief of Security, but that's not really his most important job.

His most important job is simply being Charlie.

And he's exceptionally good at it.

A Second Chance for Another Charlie

If Charlie's story has made you smile, I hope it also makes you think about the thousands of dogs waiting for someone to give them a chance.

Before buying a puppy, please consider adopting a rescue. Some of these dogs have had a difficult start in life. Some are older. Some need a little extra patience. But as Charlie has demonstrated, the dog who needs you most might just turn out to be the one you can't imagine living without.

And if adopting isn't possible, there are other ways to help. Rescue organizations depend on people willing to foster, volunteer, and contribute toward veterinary care, food, and the everyday expenses of giving these dogs a second chance.

Please take a moment to learn more about Paw-Affection Dachshund Rescue, the people who made Charlie's story possible. If you can, consider making a donation to help another dog find the kind of home Charlie eventually found.

Rescue before you buy. And if you can't rescue, help someone who can.

Somewhere out there, another Charlie is waiting.
