# Do You Think This Project Is Overkill?
## The Little Mini Who Wouldn't Quit

*One tiny screw. One forgotten computer. An entirely unreasonable solution.*

### Chapter 2 — The Little Computer on Death Row

Somewhere in my garage, sitting on a shelf, was a box.

It had been there for years.

Like many boxes in garages, it had achieved a sort of permanent residency. Nobody remembered exactly what was in it, nobody particularly needed anything from it, and every so often somebody would suggest that perhaps the entire thing should go to the dump.

Inside that box was a 2012 Mac mini.

Fourteen years old.

Forgotten.

Unemployed.

And living under what amounted to a suspended sentence.

The Mini had survived several proposed trips to the dump, mostly because nobody had gotten around to carrying the box out.

Meanwhile, a couple of years earlier, I'd had my memorable encounter with the microscopic screw securing the Vantrue F1's memory card compartment.

After spending forty-five minutes searching the garage floor for it, I'd sworn never to remove that screw again.

And I hadn't.

Instead, I had two alternatives.

I could sit in the South Carolina summer heat, close enough to the motorcycle for the camera's Lilliputian Wi-Fi to reach my phone, and wait while the videos downloaded at their own leisurely pace.

Or I could simply not bother downloading them.

Over the next couple of years, I employed both strategies.

Neither was particularly satisfying, but at least I wasn't crawling around the garage floor looking for a screw.

Then, one day, the forgotten Mac mini resurfaced.

A computer that hadn't been turned on in years.

A computer whose future had been discussed primarily in terms of municipal waste disposal.

Suddenly, it had a job opportunity.

What if I could put it in the garage, connect it to the camera, and retrieve the recordings from somewhere with air conditioning?

I brought the idea to ChatGPT.

Could we take this ancient Mac mini, install a suitable operating system, connect it to the motorcycle camera, and use it as a dedicated video-download station?

ChatGPT thought this was an excellent idea.

In retrospect, I have noticed that ChatGPT considers an alarming number of my ideas excellent.

It enthusiastically explained how we could resurrect the computer, configure networking, share files with my other Apple devices, and eventually automate the whole process.

It sounded almost effortless.

Of course, the Mini hadn't been consulted.

Neither had the keyboard.

Or the USB installer.

Or, for that matter, the laws of networking.

But the decision was made.

A fourteen-year-old computer had been granted a stay of execution.

The box was coming off the shelf.

The dump would have to wait.

And the Little Mini was about to discover that being rescued from retirement sometimes means being assigned considerably more work than you ever had before.
